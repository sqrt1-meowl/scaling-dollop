# Functional English learning path — working plan

## Product focus

- Grammar and vocabulary only
- Short learn-then-apply lessons
- Designed first for students with less than two years of English exposure
- Practical school, community, and independent-living contexts
- No speaking, listening, leaderboard, or punitive lives in the first version

## Curriculum structure

The submitted categories are source pools, not eighteen levels that students must navigate. The student experience should use a single ordered path:

1. **Get help and communicate** — pronouns, question words, `be`, classroom phrases
2. **People and routines** — present simple, common verbs, time words
3. **School day** — imperatives, classroom nouns, schedules and assignments
4. **Describe things** — `be` + adjectives, demonstratives, basic comparisons
5. **Home and food** — `have`, count/non-count nouns, `some` and `any`
6. **Places and directions** — prepositions, `there is/are`, direction language
7. **Health and safety** — `need`, `can`, `should`, emergency language
8. **Shopping and transportation** — numbers, prices, requests, past/present forms

## Lesson loop

Each lesson should take about three to five minutes and contain four to six target words plus one grammar pattern:

1. See a practical sentence and its meaning.
2. Match a word or phrase to the situation.
3. Complete a sentence using the grammar pattern.
4. Rebuild a useful sentence from word tiles.
5. Apply the language in one new school or life situation.
6. Revisit mistakes at the end and later through spaced review.

## First prototype

Unit 1: **I need help**

- Words: `I`, `you`, `help`, `need`, `understand`, `repeat`
- Grammar: `I need ___`; `I do not understand`; `Can you ___?`
- Useful chunks: “I need help.” “I don’t understand.” “Can you repeat that?”
- Activities: meaning match, choose the missing word, reorder the phrase, choose the best phrase for a classroom situation

## Data rule

Store one canonical entry per word or phrase with source, license, part of speech, meaning, example, unit, grammar objective, and difficulty. Repeated words across submitted categories should point to the same entry rather than becoming duplicate lessons.
