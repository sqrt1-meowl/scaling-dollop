Yes. For students with less than 2 years of English exposure, the target should be much more foundational than my previous list.

I checked the Oxford 3000 A1/A2 list, Cambridge Pre-A1/A1/A2 vocabulary, the general NGSL, and especially the corpus-based NGSL-Spoken. The NGSL-Spoken contains only 721 word families yet covers up to 90% of unscripted spoken English, which makes ~700–900 carefully selected words a strong starting core. The general NGSL's 2,809 words reach about 92% coverage of general English, so students can expand toward that later. Oxford likewise explicitly identifies its A1–B2 words based on importance to learners, while Cambridge maintains separate Pre-A1, A1, and A2 vocabulary sets.

The major change from my first answer is that I would not treat all of these equally. For <2-year learners, I would structure instruction approximately as:

Tier 1: ~300 words they should actively speak and understand almost automatically → Tier 2: next ~300 everyday words → Tier 3: ~200 school/community words.

That gives you roughly an 800-word functional foundation. After that, I would start moving them toward the full NGSL rather than continuing to invent isolated themed vocabulary. This approach is much closer to how corpus-based vocabulary research is intended to be used.

For your ELD program specifically, I would also teach phrases/chunks alongside these words—for example, I don't understand, What does ___ mean?, Can you help me?, Where do I go?, I need to..., I don't know, Could you repeat that? A beginner knowing 800 isolated words but being unable to combine them is much less useful than knowing the same vocabulary through reusable chunks.
